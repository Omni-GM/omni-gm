# Section-walk procedure (draft)

Use this after the briefing, for each section of the game master's own prep checklist, in their order. Each section goes: facts, then pitches, then the game master's turn. The game master leads; the AI gives first and asks rarely.

## 1. Facts

Open the section with at most five short lines of the story state that matter for it. Each line carries its source and marks claims, rumors, guesses, and plans as such. Do not repeat what the briefing already said unless this section needs it. Include a relevant secret, marked as a secret.

## 2. Pitches

Offer two or three pitches when they help this section. Skip them when the game master has already brought material, or when the section is mechanical (stat blocks, loot tables).

Each pitch is two or three lines:

- A short name.
- The situation: who wants what, where, and what lands on the table. Never how it ends or what the players choose.
- "Built on": the named facts with sources.

Pitches stay small and droppable. Never stack pitches on an unpicked pitch.

## 3. The game master's turn

End the message with a one-line hand-over, such as "Your turn." Then stop. Facts and pitches end the turn as a normal chat message, never inside a question box.

Ask a question only for:

- **A contradiction** between what the game master just said and the story state. Name both sides with sources in one line.
- **One plank question** when a main-plot gap blocks this section. Say in one sentence why you ask. The answer becomes a secret.

Never ask the game master to restate what the store or their own words already hold, or for detail the session does not need.

## 4. After the game master answers

- Record what they picked or reshaped, in their words, in the session-note draft for this section. A pitch they ignored leaves no trace.
- Background they decide that the table has not seen is a secret: intent, open to change. Never write it as story state.
- Check only what matters, one line each: does it serve the frame's goal, fit its timeframe, keep its pressure? Does it contradict a name, how an NPC was played, what the players know, a thread, or a clock? Never repair their decision yourself.
- Close with what next: the next section first, then one or two alternatives such as going back to a section or stopping with the note so far. The game master may skip a section or stop at any time.

## Example (invented)

> **Strong start: facts**
> - The harbor master expects the party at dawn [01:41:10].
> - The smugglers know someone asked about the ledger (according to the innkeeper) [00:58:12].
> - The tide clock: 2 of 4 before the chapel floods.
>
> **Pitches**
> - *Ransacked office.* The party reaches the harbor office at dawn: the door is open, papers soaked, the harbor master gone. A gull-feather token lies on the desk. Built on: the meeting [01:41:10], the smugglers' knowledge [00:58:12].
> - *The tide comes early.* A bell rings from the drowned chapel at breakfast; the tide clock moves to 3. Built on: the tide clock, the bell rumor [00:20:44].
>
> Your turn.
